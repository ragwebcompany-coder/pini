Biography

Penelope Pini

My name is Penelope Pini. I am a registered dietitian-nutritionist and Certified Health and Wellness Coach.

My interest in nutrition began with my own personal experience. As a teenager, I struggled with an unbalanced diet and a difficult relationship with food, which affected both my hormones and my weight.

As I improved my diet, I began to see how food could support my health and recovery. I also became more aware of the connection between stress, emotional eating, and the binge-eating episodes I had experienced.

These changes gave me strong motivation and gradually transformed the way I related to food. After completing my first degree in Political Science at the University of Athens, I decided to follow my growing interest in nutrition science.

I graduated from Coventry University Department of Nutrition and Applied Clinical Dietetics, where I earned a BSc Nutrition (Hons). I also completed specialist training in the dietary management of Irritable Bowel Syndrome through the “Dietary Management and the Low FODMAP Diet in IBS” programme at King’s College London.

Since 2018, I have also been certified as a Health and Wellness Coach specialising in nutrition by the National and Kapodistrian University of Athens.

Over the years, I have gained extensive experience supporting people with metabolic disorders, including insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes and hyperlipidaemia, as well as gastrointestinal conditions such as gastro-oesophageal reflux disease and irritable bowel syndrome, and autoimmune diseases.

Since 2010, I have worked in a medical practice specialising in metabolic disorders, helping clients improve their health, manage their weight and build a healthier relationship with food. My approach combines up-to-date scientific evidence, practical guidance and psychological support so that both clinical and non-clinical clients can develop habits that feel realistic and sustainable. My goal is not to put people on another restrictive diet, but to help them change the way they think about food.

After many years in practice, I have seen how exhausting repeated diets can be, especially when they lead to weight regain and feelings of failure. Moving away from diet culture does not mean giving up on health. It means choosing a more compassionate, sustainable path forward. More people are looking for peace with food and their bodies, focusing on long-term balance rather than quick, temporary results.

Thus, my philosophy is rooted in the Non-Diet Approach. I help people improve their diet quality, increase their energy, and support better health indicators through behavioural change, mindful eating, and a kinder relationship with food.
